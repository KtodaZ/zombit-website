The short version

Always On Time: Calendar Alarm is a calendar alarm app from Zombit Studios LLC ("we") for Android and Wear OS, and for iPhone. This policy covers all three; where they differ, we say so.

  • Your calendar is read on your device to schedule alarms. Alarm preferences and history stay there too.
  • Information leaves your device only for features you turn on: account sync, travel-time alarms, an AI assistant connection, feedback, and, Android only, automatic crash reports and optional usage statistics.
  • The app shows no ads; we never sell your information or use it for advertising. iPhone also has no usage statistics or crash reporting, and no subscription (Plus sells only through Google Play on Android).
  • Delete your account and synced data at Settings > Account > Manage account > Delete account. For anything else, email zombit.studios@gmail.com.
  • Our server code is open source.

What stays on your device

With your permission, the app reads calendar details, including event titles, descriptions, times, and meeting links, to schedule alarms. On Android, it reads the calendars synced to your phone. On iPhone, it reads the calendars in iOS Calendar and, if you connect one, a Google account (see below). Alarm preferences, alarm history, and the most recent calendar data are stored on your device, not on our servers.

Connecting Google Calendar

On iPhone, this is optional. In Calendars > Calendar accounts, or during setup, you can connect a Google account. Google's sign-in page opens in a secure browser sheet and asks you to allow read-only access to your calendars. The app requests one Google scope:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.readonly: to list your calendars so you can choose which ones to watch, and to read the events in the calendars you choose (titles, times, descriptions, locations, meeting links, your invitation response and organizer status) so the app can schedule, move and cancel your alarms, show your upcoming events and offer a Join button. The app never creates, changes or deletes anything in your Google Calendar.

Your Google sign-in tokens are stored in your iPhone's keychain, and calendar data is downloaded directly from Google to your iPhone. Your Google tokens are never sent to us. To update alarms soon after a calendar changes, the app asks Google to send a change notice to our relay (see Keeping iPhone alarms current). Google's notice says only that a watched calendar changed, and the relay records no event details from it.

Disconnect a Google account anytime in Calendars > Calendar accounts. The app then revokes its access with Google, deletes the tokens from your iPhone and stops watching that account's calendars. You can also remove the app's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

How we use information from Google

Always On Time's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

  • Google Calendar data is used only to provide the alarm features you see in the app.
  • It leaves your iPhone only when you turn on a feature that sends it: account sync stores your recent alarm history, including event titles (see account sync), and a connected AI assistant receives the upcoming alarms copy described in Connecting an AI assistant. With travel-time alarms on, an in-person event's address is sent to Apple Maps to work out the travel time. Feedback diagnostics leave out event titles.
  • It is not used for advertising, is not sold, and is not used to train AI or machine-learning models.
  • People do not read it, except with your permission for a support request you send, when needed for security or to investigate abuse, or to comply with the law.

If you turn on travel-time alarms, your current location or the places you save are also stored on your device; see Travel-time alarms below for how that reaches our routing service. The Wear OS companion mirrors alarm information between your paired phone and watch over Google Play services' Wearable Data Layer, with your phone staying authoritative and nothing passing through our servers. The app shows no ads and does not use AdMob or behavioral advertising. On Android, Always On Time Plus checks your subscription status with Google Play and stores that status on your device.

What our servers store, and why

These transfers happen only for features you turn on, are never used for advertising or sold, and never include your calendar content, such as titles, descriptions, locations, attendees, or meeting links, except where a feature below says so.

Keeping iPhone alarms current in the background

iOS lets a closed app do work only when a notification arrives. With Background updates on (the default), your iPhone registers with our relay, a service we run on Cloudflare, which sends the notifications that keep your alarms, notices and countdowns current. The relay stores what it needs to reach your iPhone and know when to notify it: Apple push tokens, your alarm-timing preferences, the times of upcoming alarms and countdowns, and hashed references to your alarms and watched calendars. Your Google tokens and event details such as titles, locations, attendees and meeting links stay on your iPhone. Your iPhone proves it is the genuine app with Apple's App Attest, and Cloudflare sees the IP address of each request in order to deliver it. Turning off Background updates deletes the registration and everything stored with it; if your iPhone is offline, deletion completes when it reconnects.

Optional account sync

Signing in, with Google on Android, or with Google or Sign in with Apple on iPhone, lets Google Firebase Authentication identify your account from your profile, email, and account identifier. It doesn't give the app extra access to your Gmail or Google Calendar; calendar access still uses the device's own permission. While signed in, the app syncs across your devices: alarm and appearance preferences, which calendars and events you watch, your alarm rules, and the most recent 14 days of alarm history, including event titles. It doesn't sync event descriptions, meeting links or places. Signing out stops sync on that device but keeps your saved account.

Travel-time alarms

Travel-time alarms are off by default, and you choose where trips start from: your current location or places you save. On Android, to plan a trip the app sends the starting point, the event's address, the transport mode, and the appointment time through our Google Firebase routing service to TomTom or Google (your phone's geocoder and Google Places also match addresses to coordinates). We keep short-lived counts to prevent abuse, longer hashed usage counts to size and price the service, and aggregate reliability totals with no installation or trip details; we don't store trip details, and location isn't used for advertising or sold. On iPhone, the same request goes directly to Apple Maps instead, under Apple's privacy policy, and never reaches us.

Connecting an AI assistant

This is optional and needs account sync. Once you approve a connection, such as to Claude or ChatGPT, on the Always On Time connection page, your phone saves a copy of your upcoming events and alarms for the next 30 days, including titles, times, and alarm settings, never descriptions, attendees, locations, meeting links, or travel places. Your phone also registers so the connector can reach it to apply a change or refresh the copy; on Android this uses a push token, while an iPhone registers without one and applies changes the next time it syncs. We keep which assistant is connected, a 30-day log of changes so you can undo them, and troubleshooting logs identified only by a random ID. What the assistant reads is sent on to its own provider, for example Anthropic or OpenAI, whose privacy policy then applies instead of ours. Disconnecting removes these records; an unused connection is removed automatically after 90 days. Separately, on supported Android phones, Gemini can read and change alarms directly on-device, without account sync; what it reads goes to Google as part of your Gemini conversation.

Feedback and diagnostics

When you send feedback, on Android or iPhone, we store the text you enter, your email, and any screenshots or photos you attach in Google Firebase, with a brief internal alert that contains no feedback content. If you leave "Include app logs" checked (the default), we also receive a technical activity log, with links, emails, and, on iPhone, names and event titles removed. On Android, crash and application-not-responding reports go to Firebase Crashlytics automatically, and diagnostics you choose to share can include device and app details, approximate IP-based location, device status such as battery or storage, and event titles tied to alarm scheduling activity, never serial numbers, IMEI or Wi-Fi identifiers, precise location, or your feedback text itself.

Usage statistics

This applies to Android only; the iPhone app has no usage statistics or analytics. "Share usage statistics" is on by default outside the EEA, UK, and Switzerland, and off by default there. When on, Google Analytics for Firebase receives which screens you open, whether you view or buy Plus and what led you there, whether major features like travel alarms and account sync are on, and basic device, language, and country-level location, never calendar content, event titles, or account names. Advertising identifiers and precise location are turned off.

Other requests

On Android, the app may ask DontKillMyApp.com for public battery guidance, which can reveal your device manufacturer and IP address but not calendar content.

Who processes it

Service providers process information on our behalf to run these features: Google Firebase (sign-in, sync, feedback, crash reports, analytics), Cloudflare (iPhone relay), TomTom and Google (travel routes, address lookups), Google Play (Plus billing), Apple (iPhone calendar access, Maps, push notifications), and DontKillMyApp.com (battery guidance). Once you connect one, an AI assistant's own provider, such as Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google, receives what that assistant reads and applies its own privacy terms from then on. We don't sell your information to any of them, or to anyone else.

How long we keep it, and how to delete it

We keep information only as long as the feature that created it needs it:

  • Local device data: until you delete it in the app, clear app storage, or uninstall.
  • Synced alarm history: 14 days, cleared automatically.
  • iPhone relay records: within days of the alarm or notice they relate to; the whole registration is deleted if you turn off Background updates, or 30 days after Apple reports it can no longer be reached.
  • Travel abuse checks: about 2 days; usage counts for sizing and pricing routing: 90 days; aggregate reliability totals: no fixed expiry, and hold no personal detail.
  • Assistant connection records, change logs, and request logs: up to 30 days; an unused connection is removed automatically after 90 days.
  • Feedback and diagnostics: up to 24 months, then deleted or aggregated. Usage statistics: 2 months.

To delete your account, synced settings, and history, choose Settings > Account > Manage account > Delete account, or use the account deletion page. We keep a minimal disabled account identifier afterward, only to block writes from previously issued authentication tokens. This doesn't delete your Google or Apple account, calendars, local device data, or feedback and diagnostics sent separately. For those, or any other deletion request, email zombit.studios@gmail.com with the subject "Always On Time data deletion" and enough detail (device model, approximate dates, or any identifier you have) for us to find your records.

Security

Information the app sends travels over encrypted HTTPS connections. On iPhone, your Google sign-in tokens are kept in the iOS keychain, and your saved calendar, history, and settings data is encrypted with iOS Data Protection when your iPhone has a passcode. Data we store with Google Firebase and Cloudflare is encrypted at rest; a signed-in account can reach only its own synced data, other app users can't read feedback, and sensitive identifiers such as assistant tokens are stored only as one-way hashes. Administrative access is limited to Zombit Studios. To report a security concern, email zombit.studios@gmail.com.

Permissions

On Android, the app asks for calendar access, notifications, exact alarms, full-screen alarm presentation, vibration, startup restoration, and related reliability features, plus location (including "all the time") only if you choose "Use my location" for travel-time alarms. On iPhone, it asks for full calendar access, notifications, and permission to schedule alarms (AlarmKit), plus location only for the same travel feature. Review or change these anytime in your device's settings.

Children

The app is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has supplied personal information, email zombit.studios@gmail.com.
