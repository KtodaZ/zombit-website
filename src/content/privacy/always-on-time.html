The short version

Always On Time: Calendar Alarm is a calendar alarm app from Zombit Studios LLC ("we") for Android and Wear OS, and for iPhone. This policy covers both. Where the apps differ, the section says which one it applies to.

  • Your calendar is processed on your phone to schedule alarms. Alarm preferences and history are stored on your device.
  • On Android, crash reports are sent to Firebase Crashlytics automatically. Other transfers depend on the features you use, including account sync and feedback (Google Firebase), travel-time alarms (TomTom or Google on Android, Apple Maps on iPhone), AI assistants (the assistant's provider, such as Anthropic, OpenAI or Google), battery guidance (DontKillMyApp.com) and the Wear OS companion.
  • On iPhone, you can connect a Google account so the app can read your Google Calendar. Access is read-only, and calendar data goes straight from Google to your iPhone. Background updates use our relay on Cloudflare, which never receives your calendar content. See On iPhone.
  • On Android, usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase) are optional and on by default in some regions. You can turn them off in Settings. The iPhone app has no usage statistics or crash reporting.
  • The app shows no ads. We never sell your information or use it for advertising.
  • To delete your account and synced data, go to Settings > Account > Manage account > Delete account. For feedback or diagnostics, email zombit.studios@gmail.com.
  • The cloud code that handles your information is open source.

The details are below, with the controls for each feature.

On your devices

With your permission, the app reads selected calendar details, including event titles, descriptions, times, and meeting links, to schedule alarms. Alarm preferences and history are stored on your device. On Android, the app reads the calendars synced to your phone. On iPhone, it reads the calendars in iOS Calendar and, if you connect one, your Google account (see On iPhone).

The app does not display ads, use AdMob, or use behavioral advertising.

On iPhone

The iPhone app schedules alarms on your iPhone with Apple's AlarmKit. Calendar details are processed on your iPhone. Alarm preferences, alarm history and the most recent calendar data are stored on your iPhone.

Calendars on your iPhone

With your permission, the app reads the calendars available in iOS Calendar, including accounts you added in iPhone Settings. This uses Apple's calendar access on the iPhone and sends nothing to us.

Connecting Google Calendar

This is optional. In Calendars > Calendar accounts, or during setup, you can connect a Google account. Google's sign-in page opens in a secure browser sheet and asks you to allow read-only access to your calendars. The app requests one Google scope:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.readonly: to list your calendars so you can choose which ones to watch, and to read the events in the calendars you choose (titles, times, descriptions, locations, meeting links, your invitation response and organizer status) so the app can schedule, move and cancel your alarms, show your upcoming events and offer a Join button. The app never creates, changes or deletes anything in your Google Calendar.

Your Google sign-in tokens are stored in your iPhone's keychain, and calendar data is downloaded directly from Google to your iPhone. Your Google tokens are never sent to us. To update alarms soon after a calendar changes, the app asks Google to send a change notice to our relay (described below). Google's notice says only that a watched calendar changed, and the relay records no event details from it.

Disconnect a Google account anytime in Calendars > Calendar accounts. The app then revokes its access with Google, deletes the tokens from your iPhone and stops watching that account's calendars. You can also remove the app's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

How we use information from Google

Always On Time's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

  • Google Calendar data is used only to provide the alarm features you see in the app.
  • It leaves your iPhone only when you turn on a feature that sends it: account sync stores your recent alarm history, including event titles (see account sync), and a connected AI assistant receives the upcoming alarms copy described in Connecting an AI assistant. With travel-time alarms on, an in-person event's address is sent to Apple Maps to work out the travel time. Feedback diagnostics leave out event titles.
  • It is not used for advertising, is not sold, and is not used to train AI or machine-learning models.
  • People do not read it, except with your permission for a support request you send, when needed for security or to investigate abuse, or to comply with the law.

Background updates

iOS lets an app check calendars while it is closed only when a notification arrives. With Background updates on (the default; switch it in Settings), the iPhone registers with our relay, a service we run on Cloudflare (Workers and Durable Objects). The relay sends the iPhone a quiet notification when a connected calendar changes, from time to time during your active hours, and shortly before alarms, so the app can check your calendars and update its alarms.

The relay stores your iPhone's Apple push notification token and environment, a one-way hash of a random secret created for this installation, your time zone, active hours, and whether the app checks iPhone calendars; one-way hashes of alarm identifiers with their alarm times; one-way hashes of the identifiers of Google calendar change notices, with their expiry times; and delivery bookkeeping, such as when the last notification was sent and when the iPhone last reported a check. It never receives OAuth tokens, event titles, descriptions, locations, attendees or meeting links. Its logs record installation IDs, notification types and error codes, never tokens, IP addresses or request headers. Cloudflare sees the IP address of each request in order to deliver it.

When you register, your iPhone proves it is running the genuine app with Firebase App Check, which uses Apple's App Attest. Alarm times leave the relay a day after they pass. Expired change-notice records are deleted 7 days after they expire. If Apple reports the push token is no longer valid, the installation is disabled and deleted after 30 days. Turning off Background updates deletes the installation and everything stored with it right away.

Other iPhone details

Travel-time alarms, account sync, AI assistants and feedback on iPhone are described in their sections below. The iPhone app has no ads, no usage statistics or analytics, no crash reporting and no tracking. It has no subscription or in-app purchases. Spoken alarms are created on the iPhone with Apple's built-in voices.

Travel-time alarms and location

Travel-time alarms are off by default. If you turn them on, you choose where trips start from. On iPhone, see travel on iPhone below.

Use my location. With your permission to access location all the time, the app collects your device's location, including when the app is closed or not in use, to work out when you need to leave for in-person calendar events. It reads location only when an in-person event is coming up, when a leave-time alarm rings, or when you open the app. It does not track you continuously. The most recent location is stored on your device, left out of backups and device transfers, and deleted when you turn off travel-time alarms or switch to "Use my places."

Use my places. The app never reads your device's location. Trips start from places you save, such as Home, Work or Gym, following a weekly schedule of where you usually are. Saved places and their map coordinates are stored on your device. You can also save places when using your location.

Who we share this with. To plan a trip, the app sends the starting point (your location or a saved place), the calendar destination, the transport mode, and the appointment time through our Google Firebase routing service to one of these route providers: TomTom (for driving, cycling and walking) or Google (for public transit, or when TomTom is unavailable). First, your phone's built-in geocoder, which on most phones is provided by Google, matches the event address to map coordinates. Address search while saving places uses Google Places. Routing requests happen only for physical events without a detected meeting link. Location is not used for advertising and is not sold or shared for any other purpose.

To prevent abuse, the routing service checks that requests come from a genuine copy of the app (Firebase App Check with Google Play Integrity) and counts each installation's daily route requests by its Firebase installation ID. Those counts are kept for about two days. The routing service also keeps daily totals of requests, provider errors and active installations to monitor reliability and service costs. These aggregate totals contain no installation identifiers, addresses, coordinates or trip details and may be kept after the per-installation counts expire. The routing service does not store trip details. If routing is unavailable, the app keeps the last successful plan or falls back to the event's existing alarms.

On iPhone. You can use your location (while using the app, or all the time so leave times stay current when the app is closed) or saved places. Your location and saved places stay on your iPhone and are shared only with Apple Maps for travel times. To plan a trip, the app sends the starting point, the event's address, the transport mode and the arrival time to Apple Maps (MapKit), which also matches event addresses and place searches to map coordinates. Apple's privacy policy applies to those requests. The iPhone app does not use our routing service, TomTom or Google for travel, and does not send your location to us.

Always On Time Plus subscription

Always On Time Plus is an optional subscription sold through Google Play on Android. The iPhone app has no subscription. Google Play processes payment and your Google account details under Google's terms; we don't receive your payment details. The app reads from Google Play whether your subscription is active and stores that status on your device. Manage or cancel the subscription in Google Play.

Optional account sync

When you choose Sign in with Google in Settings, Google Firebase Authentication processes your Google profile, email address, and account identifier to identify your Always On Time account. Signing in does not give the app access to your Gmail or Google Calendar through Google's web APIs; calendar access still uses the phone's calendar permission.

On iPhone, Google's sign-in page opens in a secure browser sheet and the app asks only for your basic identity (the openid and email scopes). This is separate from connecting Google Calendar: account sync works the same way whether or not you connect a calendar, and syncs the same information as on Android. The device label on iPhone history is "iPhone".

On iPhone you can instead choose Sign in with Apple. Apple confirms your identity and Google Firebase Authentication receives an Apple account identifier and the email address you choose to share, which can be a private relay address from Apple's Hide My Email. The app asks Apple for your email only, not your name. Android offers only Sign in with Google, so an account made with Sign in with Apple syncs between iPhones only. When you delete an account made with Sign in with Apple, the app or the account deletion page also asks Apple to revoke the app's sign-in access.

While you're signed in, the app saves these to Firebase Cloud Firestore: supported alarm and appearance preferences, selected-calendar choices, calendar and recurring-series alarm rules, individual event overrides, and whether alarms are paused. Calendar and event matching identifiers are hashed, but they are still account data, not anonymous data. These settings come back when you reinstall or change phones, and they update your other signed-in phones when those phones can connect. Offline edits are queued on the phone. If two phones change the same setting at the same time, the last accepted upload wins.

The app also saves the most recent 14 days of calendar alarm history: event titles, event and alarm times, dismiss/snooze actions, lead times, and a device model label. History from other phones is shown for reference and never recreates or triggers an alarm. Account sync does not upload event descriptions, meeting URLs, destinations, saved places, custom sound files or their device paths, local voice identifiers, permissions, or diagnostic-consent choices.

Google Firebase processes this information on our behalf for account management, backup, and synchronization. Signing out stops sync on that phone and keeps its local settings and history; it does not delete the saved account. A daily cleanup removes cloud history older than 14 days, so deletion can take up to one extra day. Other account settings stay until you delete your Always On Time account.

To delete saved account settings, synced history, and your Always On Time authentication identity, choose Settings > Account > Manage account > Delete account, or use the account deletion page. You may need to sign in again to confirm. We keep a minimal disabled account identifier to block writes from previously issued authentication tokens. This does not delete your Google or Apple account, your calendars, local phone data, or feedback and diagnostics you sent separately. To delete those records, follow the deletion instructions below.

Connecting an AI assistant

This is optional and needs account sync. To connect an assistant such as Claude or ChatGPT, you sign in on the Always On Time connection page the way you do in the app, with Google or, for an account made on iPhone, Sign in with Apple, and approve the connection. The cloud connector collects the information below only after you approve a connection. Gemini on this phone works separately, as described below.

  • Upcoming alarms copy. Your phone saves to your account a copy of upcoming events and alarms on watched calendars for the next 30 days: event titles, start and end times, whether events repeat or are all-day, your invitation response or organizer status, and hashed matching identifiers. It also includes all calendar names, their account types, watched state and alarm settings; alarm times, lead times, sources and status; whether and until when alarms are paused; and the phone's model label and time zone. It never includes event descriptions, attendee lists, locations, meeting links, travel origins or destinations, or sound choices. Each update from the phone replaces the copy.
  • Phone registration. Your phone registers a Firebase Cloud Messaging token, its model label, app version and supported features, so the connector can ask it to apply a change, refresh the copy, or show a test notification. An iPhone registers its label, app version and supported features without a push token, and applies an assistant's changes the next time the app syncs your account.
  • Connection records. We store which assistant is connected, the address it returns to after approval, when it connected and was last used, and its access tokens, stored only as one-way hashes. Changes the assistant makes are logged with the previous values for 30 days so you can undo them. Connector request logs record which action was taken, random IDs, the outcome and timing, never event titles or settings contents. We keep them for troubleshooting for up to 30 days. They identify your account only by a random log ID, never your name, email address or account ID. Google Cloud, which hosts the connector, also keeps standard request logs, including IP address and app or browser type, for up to 30 days.

A connected assistant can read the upcoming alarms copy, your synced alarm settings and 14 days of alarm history, and it can change your alarm settings. It cannot read or change your calendar, and signing in on the connection page does not give the assistant your Google or Apple account.

What the assistant reads is sent to the assistant's provider (for example Anthropic or OpenAI). From then on, that provider's privacy policy and terms apply to it, not this policy.

The connector also keeps anonymous daily totals, such as how many assistants are connected and how often each feature is used, with no account identifiers or content.

Disconnect anytime in Settings > Connected assistants or in your assistant. Disconnecting the last assistant deletes the upcoming alarms copy, phone registrations and change log right away. Connections unused for 90 days are removed. Deleting your Always On Time account removes its connections, tokens, upcoming alarms copy, phone registrations, change log and the record linking your random log ID to you. After that, request logs that remain until they expire can no longer be connected to you. Anonymous daily totals are kept without account identifiers.

Gemini on this phone. On Android 16 and later, Gemini can check and change your alarms on the phone itself when you ask it to. This needs no account sync or cloud connector. If you use account sync, supported settings changed through Gemini sync to your account like changes made in the app. The app answers Gemini with the same kinds of information listed above (event titles and times, calendar names and alarm settings, never descriptions, attendee lists, locations, meeting links or travel places). Gemini sends what it reads to Google as part of your Gemini conversation, where Google's privacy policy applies.

A local change log lets you undo recent Gemini changes for up to 30 days; older entries may be replaced sooner. Expired entries are hidden and cleared the next time the log is updated. The log is left out of backups and device transfers. Gemini access is on by default on supported phones and responds when you ask Gemini to use the app. Turn it off anytime in Settings > Connected assistants.

Wear OS companion

If you use the Wear OS companion, the alarm information needed to show and operate an alarm passes between your paired phone and watch through Google Play services' Wearable Data Layer. The phone remains authoritative for calendar data, settings, and history.

Feedback and diagnostics

When you send feedback from the app's Feedback page, on Android or iPhone, the app sends the text you enter, a required email address, the app version, the platform (Android or iPhone), and a server-generated submission time to our Firebase Cloud Firestore database. Optional screenshots or photos are uploaded to Firebase Cloud Storage under a user-scoped path that other app users cannot read. On iPhone, photos are re-saved as JPEG images before upload, which leaves out their location and other photo details. The app signs in to Firebase to authorize the submission, and that account identifier is part of the storage path of any attached files. In some Android app versions, if you are signed in with Google for account sync, it is the same identifier as your synced account. Otherwise, in newer Android versions and always on iPhone (whether or not you signed in with Google or Apple), it is a random identifier from Firebase Anonymous Authentication that is not linked to your account. Files uploaded for feedback that is never submitted are deleted after about a day.

After you submit, a trusted Firebase backend sends us a short alert in a private Discord channel saying that feedback arrived. The alert contains only the app version, the platform, and the number of attached images and logs. It does not include your feedback text, email address, images, or logs.

The feedback form also has an optional Include app logs checkbox, which is checked by default. When it is checked, the app attaches a text export of Always On Time diagnostic entries from the last 24 hours. Android system logcat is not included because ordinary apps cannot read it. Links and email addresses are removed from this export before upload. The log is stored privately in Firebase Cloud Storage for us to review and is not included in the Discord alert. On iPhone, the same checkbox attaches a text file with alarm scheduling counts and states, the app and iOS versions, and a technical event log; links, email addresses, phone numbers, account names and event titles are left out.

Firebase Crashlytics receives crash, non-fatal error, and application-not-responding reports from the Android app. Earlier Android versions had an off-by-default Remote log streaming developer option; it has been removed, and its logging service no longer receives data.

Android diagnostic records can include:

  • a Firebase Installation ID and a short identifier for the current app session;
  • device manufacturer and model, device build family fields, Android release/API level, security patch level, app version/build type, supported CPU ABIs, locale, IP-derived approximate location, timestamps, and log severity;
  • network availability, internet validation, metered state, and transport type; battery percentage, charging, and power-save state; available/total memory and low-memory status; and available/total app-storage capacity in an explicit diagnostics export or when a feedback or diagnostic transport failure is recorded;
  • app interactions and technical diagnostic messages; and
  • calendar-event titles and internal identifiers when the app records scheduling, synchronization, cancellation, alarm, or restoration activity.

Failure context does not include serial numbers, Android IDs, IMEI/SIM identifiers, Wi-Fi or Bluetooth names, precise location, feedback text, contact email, or attachment contents. A feedback submission you choose to send still contains the information described above.

We don't sell this information. Feedback is used to improve the app and respond to reported problems. Diagnostics are used only for app-health analytics, troubleshooting, security, and reliability improvements. Service providers, including Google Firebase and our hosting provider, process information on our behalf. Information is sent over HTTPS.

Usage statistics

This applies to Android only. The iPhone app does not include Google Analytics and sends no usage statistics.

The Share usage statistics setting in Settings is on by default, except in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland (or when the app cannot tell the region). There it stays off unless you turn it on during setup or in Settings. When it is on, the app uses Google Analytics for Firebase to send:

  • which app screens you open, using fixed screen names;
  • when you view the Always On Time Plus screen, start a purchase or buy Plus, and which part of the app or which assistant link led you there;
  • whether Plus, travel alarms, account sync and spoken alarms are turned on; and
  • a random app-instance identifier generated by Google Analytics, the app version, Android version, device category, language, and country-level location derived from your IP address.

It never includes calendar or event details, event titles, calendar or account names, email addresses, places, or routes. The advertising ID, Android ID, Google signals, ads personalization, city-level location and detailed device collection are turned off. Google acts as a service provider, and we have set Google Analytics to keep this data for 2 months. Turning the setting off stops collection right away and resets the analytics data stored on your device.

Other network requests

On Android, the app may request public, manufacturer-specific battery guidance from DontKillMyApp.com. The request can reveal the device manufacturer and IP address, but it does not intentionally include calendar events or meeting links.

Retention and deletion

We may keep feedback and diagnostic records for up to 24 months, then delete them or keep them only in aggregated form.

To request deletion of remotely collected feedback or diagnostics, email zombit.studios@gmail.com with the subject "Always On Time data deletion." Include the device model and approximate dates of use, plus the feedback text, Firebase Installation ID, or diagnostic session ID if you have them, so we can find matching records. We will respond within a reasonable period and will explain if records cannot be reliably identified from the details provided.

Local app data stays on your devices until you delete it in the app, clear the app's storage, or uninstall the app. Wear alarm records are removed or replaced as alarms are completed, cancelled, or updated. Background-update records for an iPhone are kept as described in Background updates.

Permissions

The app asks for the permissions it needs for calendar access, notifications, exact alarms, full-screen alarm presentation, vibration, startup restoration, and related reliability features. It also asks for location (including all the time) if you choose "Use my location" for travel-time alarms. You can review or revoke permissions in Android system settings.

On iPhone, the app asks for full access to your calendars, notifications, and permission to schedule alarms (AlarmKit). It asks for location, while using the app or all the time, only if you choose to use your location for travel-time alarms. You can review or change these in iPhone Settings.
