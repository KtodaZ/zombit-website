The short version

This policy covers the PhotoVault app for Android (its Google Play name is Photo Vault), made by Zombit Studios LLC ("we"). The app is provided "AS IS".

  • There is no PhotoVault account and no PhotoVault server. We never receive your photos, videos, albums, passcode, master password, or vault key.
  • Photos and videos you add are encrypted on your phone and kept in the app's private storage, not in your phone's gallery.
  • Google Drive backup is optional. If you connect it, PhotoVault uploads only encrypted data, to a hidden app folder in your own Google Drive. The permission it asks for covers only that folder, not your other Drive files.
  • The app has no advertising, analytics, or crash-reporting services. Its only network connections are to Google, for Google Drive.
  • A diagnostics report is created only when you export it, and it reaches us only if you send it to us yourself.
  • Uninstalling removes the vault from your phone. You can delete PhotoVault's data from Google Drive in Drive's settings. To ask us anything, email zombit.studios@gmail.com.

The details are below.

What stays on your phone

Each photo and video you add is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it is stored; videos are encrypted in separately authenticated chunks so they can play without a decrypted copy. The files sit in PhotoVault's app-private storage, which other apps can't read, and are never written to your phone's gallery or shared media storage. Photos are decrypted in memory for viewing, and videos are decrypted piece by piece as they play.

The vault's catalog (such as album names, favorites, Trash, the date each item was added, and whether it was captured in the app or imported) is kept in an encrypted database. The keys for your media and catalog are protected by Android Keystore on your phone. Your six-digit passcode is stored only as a salted, one-way hash, in encrypted storage. PhotoVault asks for the passcode every time you open it, blocks screenshots and screen recording, and shows a neutral cover on the recent-apps screen.

PhotoVault opts out of Android's own backup and device-to-device transfer, so your phone's Google backup does not include the vault.

The app also keeps a small, encrypted log of recent app events on your phone (times, error codes, and status values, never media or names), which the Diagnostics screen shows. See Exports and diagnostics.

Camera, imports, and permissions

The in-app camera asks for camera permission, and microphone permission so videos can record sound, when you first open it. A capture is written to a temporary file in app-private storage, encrypted, and the temporary file is deleted. The app does not request location, contacts, or access to all your photos.

To import, PhotoVault uses Android's photo picker, or you share photos and videos into it from another app. Either way, it can read only the items you choose. Importing copies them into the vault; PhotoVault does not move or delete the originals.

PhotoVault also uses the internet and network-state permissions, to reach Google Drive and to check for a connection (and, by default, for Wi-Fi) before backing up.

Optional Google Drive backup

You can use PhotoVault without Google Drive. If you choose to connect it, Google shows its own account choice and consent screen. PhotoVault asks for one permission, Google Drive's app-data scope (drive.appdata), which Google describes as letting the app "see, create, and delete its own configuration data in your Google Drive". It covers only a hidden folder that belongs to PhotoVault. PhotoVault cannot see your other Drive files, and the folder does not appear among them.

Once connected, PhotoVault uploads to that folder:

  • encrypted copies of your photos and videos;
  • the encrypted catalog, so the vault can be restored or kept in step on your other phones signed in to the same Google account; and
  • if you set a master password and send it to Drive, a recovery copy of your vault key, sealed on your phone with your master password (Argon2id and AES-256-GCM).

Everything is encrypted before it leaves your phone, and the files have random names. Google can see how many files there are, their sizes, and when they change, but not what they contain. Your master password never leaves your phone, and we can't reset it: without it, the recovery copy can't be opened.

Automatic backup runs only while the vault is unlocked and, unless you change it, only on Wi-Fi. You can turn it off on the backup screen. Restoring or setting up the vault on another phone downloads the encrypted data and decrypts it on that phone.

The data is stored by Google in your Google account, counts against your Google storage, and is subject to Google's Privacy Policy. We don't receive a copy.

Google user data

When you connect Google Drive, PhotoVault accesses only:

  • the files PhotoVault itself creates in its hidden app-data folder, described above; and
  • your Google Drive permission ID (Google's identifier for your account in Drive), read when you connect and whenever Google access is renewed, to check which account is connected.

PhotoVault does not request your email address, name, profile picture, contacts, or any other Google data. From the permission ID it keeps only a one-way fingerprint, in encrypted storage on your phone, so the vault can't be connected to a different Google account by mistake. Google's access token is kept in memory only while the vault is unlocked, and is cleared when the vault locks; PhotoVault does not store a long-lived refresh token.

This data is used only to back up, restore, and sync your own vault. It stays on your phone and in your Google account: it is never sent to us or to anyone else, never sold, never used for advertising, and never used to develop, improve, or train artificial intelligence or machine learning models. No person reads it.

PhotoVault's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs, including Google Workspace scopes, will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Exports and diagnostics

When you export a photo or video, PhotoVault decrypts that one item and saves it where you choose with Android's file picker. The exported copy is not encrypted and is no longer protected by PhotoVault.

The Diagnostics screen can export a text report: status counts, self-check results, and recent app events (time, component, operation, error code, attempt, and duration). It never includes media, file names, paths, links, email addresses, account or provider IDs, tokens, keys, passwords, or crash traces. It is saved only where you choose, and PhotoVault keeps no copy. The app never sends it anywhere; if you email it to us for help, we use it only to answer you, and we delete it when you ask.

Third parties

The app contains no advertising, analytics, or crash-reporting services, and we don't sell or share your information. The only outside services involved are Google's: Google Drive storage, and Google Play services, which shows the Google account choice and consent screen. Google Play also distributes the app. Each operates under Google's Privacy Policy.

Your choices and deletion

  • Delete an item: move it to Trash, then delete it permanently. It is removed from your phone right away, and PhotoVault then tries to delete its encrypted copy from Google Drive after a later successful backup or sync.
  • Disconnect Google Drive: in the app's Connected storage screen, choose Disconnect. PhotoVault stops backup work and revokes its Google access through Google. Your vault stays on your phone, and the encrypted data already in Drive stays there. You can also remove PhotoVault's access from your Google Account's third-party connections page.
  • Delete PhotoVault's data from Google Drive: on a computer, open Google Drive, choose Settings › Manage apps, find PhotoVault (or Photo Vault), and choose Options › Delete hidden app data. This removes every encrypted copy, including any extra copies left after reconnecting Drive.
  • Delete everything on your phone: uninstall PhotoVault. This removes the vault, its keys, and its settings from your phone. Export anything you want to keep first: without the vault key, the data can't be recovered.

We hold no copy of your vault, so there is nothing for us to delete on our side. If you emailed us, you can ask us to delete those messages at zombit.studios@gmail.com.

Children

PhotoVault is not directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, email zombit.studios@gmail.com and we will delete it.

Security

PhotoVault is built so that only someone with your phone and your passcode can open the vault, and only someone with your master password can open the recovery copy. For the best protection, set a screen lock on your phone: without one, the vault key can't be tied to your phone's lock screen. We can't reset a forgotten passcode or master password. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure.
