## What to look for in a visual timer app

For children, these features matter most:

- **A clear visual.** A colored area that shrinks as time passes, readable without numbers.
- **Child-friendly controls.** Can your child start it alone?
- **A way to stop fiddling.** A lock, so the time doesn't get changed mid-routine.
- **Ads and upsells.** A timer you hand to a four-year-old shouldn't show ads.
- **Presets.** One-tap timers for routines you repeat every day.
- **Price model.** One-time purchase, subscription, or free with ads.

## Physical Time Timer vs an app

Time Timer also sells physical timers. Prices on timetimer.com on October 17, 2026:

| Time Timer Original 3" / 8" / 12" | $29.95 / $38.95 / $43.95 |

| Time Timer MOD Education Edition | $36.95 |
| Time Timer PLUS (5, 20, 60 or 120 minutes) | $40.95 |
| Time Timer TWIST | $22.95 |
| Time Timer Watch | $85.95 |

The Original and MOD models are 60-minute disk timers that run silently (no ticking), with an end alert you can switch off. They run on AA batteries: two for the 8" Original, one for the MOD. The TWIST is different: a small digital timer that goes up to 90 minutes. Several Time Timer product pages say the timer includes limited-time premium access to the Time Timer app (one page says one year).

**A physical timer is better when:**

- You want no screen in the room, or the child shouldn't handle a phone or tablet.
- The timer needs to stay in one place, like a classroom wall or a bathroom shelf.
- Several children need to see it at once from across a room.

**An app is better when:**

- You want it free or cheap, or need timers in several places.
- You use presets for different routines.
- You want a choice of end sounds, or need it on the go (in the car, at a grandparent's house, in a waiting room).
- You already have a tablet set aside for your child.

Many families end up with both: a physical timer at home for daily routines and an app on the phone for everywhere else.
