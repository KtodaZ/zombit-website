The best-known visual timer app is the official Time Timer app: free to download, with a $9.95 unlock on iOS, and available on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and Android. If you want something different, Visual Countdown Timer adds pictures and colour stages, Mouse Timer and Little Timer use playful characters, and Montessori Visual Timer keeps it minimal with no ads and a PIN lock. A physical Time Timer costs $22.95 to $43.95 for the main models and suits classrooms that want no screen at all.

Below is how the main options compare, with prices and ratings as listed on October 17, 2026.

## What to look for in a visual timer app

Before comparing apps, it helps to know what matters most for children:

- **A clear visual.** A coloured area that shrinks as time passes, readable without numbers.
- **Child-friendly controls.** Can your child start it alone?
- **A way to stop fiddling.** A lock, so the time doesn't get changed mid-routine.
- **Ads and upsells.** A timer you hand to a four-year-old shouldn't show ads.
- **Presets.** One-tap timers for routines you repeat every day.
- **Price model.** One-time purchase, subscription, or free with ads.

## Which app suits which family

**You want the classic, everywhere.** Time Timer is the original brand, and its app covers more devices than any other here, including Mac and Apple Watch. Teachers who already have a physical Time Timer will recognise the red disk instantly. The free version is limited; on iOS a $9.95 purchase unlocks everything.

**Your child responds to pictures and colour.** Visual Countdown Timer has by far the most ratings in this list and uses green, amber and red stages plus a picture that reveals itself. Visual Timer for Kids also reveals a picture, with a large picture library, though its subscription pricing adds up if you only need a timer.

**Your child loves characters.** Mouse Timer and Little Timer turn the countdown into a small animation. Both are free to start. Note that the iOS version of Little Timer was last updated in 2021, according to its listing.

**You just need a simple Android timer.** Visual Timer - Countdown is free, highly rated with over 24,000 reviews, and has no ads label.

**You want calm, no ads, and locked controls.** That's what we built Montessori Visual Timer for. A coloured section of the clock face shrinks as time passes, with nothing else on screen. Quick timers start routines like Brush Teeth or Cleanup in one tap, a PIN keeps small hands from changing the time, and it can lock automatically when a timer starts. Both store listings say no data is collected. It's newer than the others, with only a few ratings so far, and it doesn't do pictures or characters. If your child is motivated by those, one of the apps above may suit them better.

The physical timers are silent (no ticking), use a red 60-minute disk, and run on AA batteries (two for the 8" Original, one for the MOD). The end alert can be switched off. Time Timer says its physical timers come with access to its premium app features, though the length of that access varies between product pages.

**A physical timer is better when:**

- You want no screen in the room, or the child shouldn't handle a phone or tablet.
- The timer needs to stay in one place, like a classroom wall or a bathroom shelf.
- Several children need to see it at once from across a room.

**An app is better when:**

- You want it free or cheap, or need timers in several places.
- You use presets for different routines.
- You want a choice of end sounds, or need it on the go (in the car, at a grandparent's house, in a waiting room).
- You already have a tablet set aside for your child.

Many families end up with both: a physical timer at home for daily routines and an app on the phone for everywhere else.

## The bottom line

There's no single best visual timer. Time Timer is the safest all-rounder and the only one with a matching physical product. Visual Countdown Timer offers the most visual features. Character apps like Mouse Timer and Little Timer win over some reluctant kids.
