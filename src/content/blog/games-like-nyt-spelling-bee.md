## How Spelling Bee-style puzzles work

You get seven letters, usually arranged in a hive or grid, and you make as many words as you can. In NYT's version, per its own How to Play screen:

- Words must be at least 4 letters long.
- Every word must include the center letter.
- Letters can be used more than once.
- 4-letter words are worth 1 point, and longer words earn 1 point per letter.
- Each puzzle has at least one pangram, a word that uses every letter, worth 7 extra points.
- A new puzzle comes out daily at 3 a.m. ET.

Your score moves you up a ladder of ranks. NYT bases ranks on a percentage of the points available in that day's puzzle, climbing from Beginner, Good Start and Moving Up through Good, Solid, Nice, Great and Amazing to Genius, with Queen Bee for finding every word. Most of the alternatives below use a similar ladder, though the rank names vary.

Most alternatives copy these rules closely and differ in price, ads, how many puzzles you get, and how much you can change the rules.

## Which one to pick

**You're playing with kids, or you want a calm daily puzzle with no ads: Word Garden (our pick).** Word Garden lets you **switch off the center-letter requirement** and **drop the minimum to three letters**, and its scoring is designed to keep younger players motivated, so they find enough words to stay interested. Google Play shows it as **Teacher Approved for ages 6+**, and both store listings say **no data is collected**. It's **free with no ads and no in-app purchases**, on iPhone, iPad, Android and the web. None of the other listings we checked describe settings made for children, though Pangram's adjustable minimum word length could serve a similar purpose.

**You want the original puzzle.** NYT Spelling Bee is hand-edited (the puzzle page credits editor Sam Ezersky), and NYT runs a Spelling Bee forum where solvers swap tips on each day's puzzle. If you play every day, the Games subscription also unlocks the archive of past puzzles and NYT's other games. It is the most-rated app on this list by a wide margin.

**You want to play more than once a day.** SpellBee and Spelling Queen Pangrams both offer unlimited puzzles alongside a daily one, and both are free with ads. SpellBee also has a free web version at spellbee.org with a daily puzzle and an unlimited mode. Spelling Queen adds an Apple Watch version and a short-game option for quicker rounds.

**You don't want ads.** Word Garden has **no ads and no in-app purchases** on any platform. On Android, Spelling Bee – Unlimited also describes itself as free, ad-free and offline, with no accounts.

**You like tweaking the rules.** Pangram on iOS lets you set the number of center and outer letters and the minimum word length, and offers Merriam-Webster word lists as a paid add-on. It also has shared games and leaderboards.

**You'd rather play in a browser.** spellsbee.com, spellbee.org and Word Garden's web version (wordgarden.zombit.io) all run without an install.

## Tips for scoring higher in any pangram puzzle

These work in NYT Spelling Bee and every game above.

1. **Hunt the pangram early.** It's worth the biggest bonus, and spotting it often reveals word families you'll use later. Look for a common ending among the letters, then build backward.
2. **Work through prefixes and suffixes.** If the letters allow RE-, UN-, IN-, -ING, -ED, -ER, -ION or -LY, add them to every base word you find. One root can give you four or five words.
3. **Remember letters repeat.** Doubled letters (like LL, EE, OO, TT) are easy to miss because the hive shows each letter once.
4. **Go letter by letter.** Pick one starting letter and list everything you can think of before moving on. It's slower but misses less.
5. **Shuffle and step away.** Rearranging the letters breaks the patterns your eyes are stuck on, and coming back after a break usually turns up a few more words.
6. **For kids, lower the bar first.** Allowing three-letter words and dropping the center-letter rule (where a game allows it) gives younger players early wins. Tighten the rules as they get faster.

## A note for spelling bee families

Pangram puzzles build vocabulary and word-finding, but they aren't spelling bee practice. A school spelling bee asks children to spell a word they hear aloud. If that's your goal, Scripps, which runs the National Spelling Bee, offers a free study app called [Word Club](https://spellingbee.com/word-club).
